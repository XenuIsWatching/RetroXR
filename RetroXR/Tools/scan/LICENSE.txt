nunchuk_body.tri, nunchuk_c.tri, nunchuk_z.tri

What is here, and what is not
-----------------------------
The scans themselves are five STL meshes totalling 250 MB at about 2.5 million
triangles each, and they are NOT in this repository. Three small meshes derived
from them are:

  nunchuk_body.tri   the two shell halves joined and reduced 5 000 000 -> 8 000
                     triangles by Blender's Collapse decimator
  nunchuk_c.tri      the C key, cropped to its cap plus a 5.5 mm skirt
  nunchuk_z.tri      the Z key, likewise

    blender --background --python Tools/glb/decimate_stl.py -- \
        --in <scan>/Top.stl --in <scan>/Bottom.stl --out /tmp/shell.stl --target 8000
    python Tools/art/nunchuk_scan_keys.py --scan <scan dir> --shell /tmp/shell.stl

The moulding seam and the stick's octagonal gate need no separate parts any
more: both are geometry the shell already has.

The analog STICK and the cord's strain relief are NOT from the scan -- the
download does not contain them -- and are still drawn procedurally by
RetroXR/Tools/gen_nunchuk.gd.

Format
------
"NCTR", then two little-endian uint32 (vertex count, triangle count), then
three float32 per vertex and three uint32 per triangle. Metres.

The shell is in the controller's own frame, centred on its bounding box. Each
key is in ITS own frame -- base at y = 0, cap out along +Y, centred on x -- and
nunchuk.tscn carries the transform that places it, so that nunchuk.gd's press
animation has a local axis to travel along. gen_nunchuk.gd reads all three and
bakes them to Scenes/Objects/controllers/wii/nunchuk_{body,c,z}.res.
