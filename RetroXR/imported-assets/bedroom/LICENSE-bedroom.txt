props/throw_pillows_01_*  (+ props/textures/)
    "Throw Pillows 01" by Serhii Khromov — Poly Haven
    https://polyhaven.com/a/throw_pillows_01
    6.4k tris across TWO separate pillow meshes, so one download scatters as
    several cushions: each scene instance hides one and re-skins the other.
    Its diffuse is dropped — the bold chevron print lives only there, at 0.772
    mean saturation, while the normal map carries just folds and the roughness
    just weave. Flat albedo plus those two gives plain fabric.

props/potted_plant_04_*  (+ props/textures/)
    "Potted Plant 04" by James Ray Cock — Poly Haven
    https://polyhaven.com/a/potted_plant_04
    8.9k tris, 0.27 m tall. Chosen over Potted Plant 01 and 02 on cost, not
    looks: those are 96k and 70k triangles, either of them more than every other
    object in this room put together, and both are floor plants rather than the
    desk-sized one wanted here.

materials/Plaster001_*
    "Plaster 001" — ambientCG, surface photogrammetry
    https://ambientcg.com/a/Plaster001
    Painted drywall, tagged "clean". Only Color, NormalGL and Roughness are kept:
    the pack also ships NormalDX (wrong convention for Godot — every bump would
    read as a dent) and Displacement (nothing here uses parallax, and it is
    expensive on the mobile backend).

    Replaced PaintedPlaster017, which had a branching crack carved into its
    normal map as well as its albedo — raking lamplight picked it out and the
    walls read as damaged. Almost the entire PaintedPlaster series is tagged
    "broken", "old" or "cracks"; the Plaster series is where the clean ones are.

materials/Carpet016_*
    "Carpet 016" — ambientCG
    https://ambientcg.com/a/Carpet016
    Same reasons for dropping NormalDX and Displacement, but FOUR maps are kept
    here, not three: this is the one set in the room that ships an
    AmbientOcclusion map, and pile is exactly the deep micro-relief a tiling AO
    does describe. The Plaster series ships none at all, and would not want one
    — a tiling AO knows nothing about where a room's corners are, so the wall
    and corner shading is analytic instead (Shaders/pbr_surface.gdshader).

materials/Wood049_*
    "Wood 049" — ambientCG
    https://ambientcg.com/a/Wood049
    Tagged oak/furniture/interior. The door LEAF only — surface 4 of the kit's
    single mesh — on BOTH doors, the room's and the closet's. Each jamb and
    casing keeps the room's painted trim. Color, NormalGL and Roughness kept, as
    with the plaster.

    The doors are WHITE, and this is still a wood set on purpose: pbr_surface's
    paint_over covers the oak's colour while its grain, mill marks and roughness
    variation keep coming through the normal and roughness maps. A painted panel
    door is not a uniform surface, it just is not brown.

materials/Grass005_*
    "Grass 005" — ambientCG
    https://ambientcg.com/a/Grass005
    Tagged clean/lawn/short. The front lawn, which is the single biggest surface
    in the window: it used to be one flat Color(0.11, 0.14, 0.09) across a
    140 x 140 plane. Four maps kept — this set ships an AmbientOcclusion and
    grass is exactly the deep micro-relief one describes.

materials/Road007_*
    "Road 007" — ambientCG
    https://ambientcg.com/a/Road007
    Tagged center/dashed/lane: two solid edge lines and a dashed centre line are
    IN the texture, so the street needs no extra geometry to be marked out. The
    tile has to land across the road exactly once — see Mat_road in
    BedroomScene.tscn for the swap_uv / tiles_per_metre / uv_offset that does it.

materials/Rocks022_*
    "Rocks 022" — ambientCG
    https://ambientcg.com/a/Rocks022
    Exposed aggregate: the driveway, the front walk and both sidewalks. Filed
    under gravel/pebbles and named Rocks, but the preview is textbook exposed
    aggregate — rounded stones set densely in mortar, the finish a 90s drive and
    sidewalk have. ambientCG has nothing tagged "aggregate" at all, so this was
    found by looking at previews rather than searching tags.

    Four maps: its AmbientOcclusion earns its place the way the carpet's and the
    lawn's do, because pebble-in-mortar is exactly the deep micro-relief a tiling
    AO describes. It replaced Concrete048, which read as sand at this tint and
    never looked like a driveway.

materials/Ground048_*
