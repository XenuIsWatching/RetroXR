What was done to it, by Tools/glb/prepare_n64_pad.py
----------------------------------------------------

Re-run that script to rebuild this file from the source; it is deterministic and
verifies its own work (`--verify`).

  TRADEMARKS REMOVED. Two marks, cleared from all three texture maps:

    - the "Nintendo(R)" wordmark on the front face, inside its moulded badge
    - the Nintendo 64 "N" logo and the "NUS-005 ... 64 CONTROLLER" legend
      moulded into the underside

  Each appears in EVERY map, which is the trap this asset sets. The wordmark is
  embossed in the normal map, cut into the roughness map at full contrast, AND
  printed into the base colour at about six grey levels out of 255 — invisible
  in a min/max summary of the region, and perfectly legible in a render as flat
  grey lettering. Clearing the normal map alone removes the moulding and leaves
  the printing exactly as readable as before.

  The badge's OUTLINE is deliberately kept and only its interior cleared, so the
  face still carries the moulded oval a real pad has: an empty badge reads as a
  blank plate rather than as a scrubbed one. The underside legend has no such
  frame and went entirely.

  Cleared regions are RECONSTRUCTED from the surface around them (normalised
  convolution plus matched grain), not patched from elsewhere on the map and not
  filled flat.

  GEOMETRY. The source ships the body twice — `Body` at 2102 triangles and a
  3520-triangle `N64_Controller_BACKUP` occupying the identical AABB. Both would
  draw, z-fighting the shell against itself. The duplicate is dropped, leaving
  3520 triangles total across 13 meshes.

  The four Sketchfab/FBX wrapper nodes are collapsed into one root (their two
  rotations cancel to identity, checked before discarding them), and the
  centimetre-scale geometry is baked to metres — NOT left to the importer's
  root scale, which would leave every button's local transform in centimetres
  and put the animation's press depths out by a factor of 100.

  TEXTURES. The three maps arrive at 4096x4096, ~19 MB of GLB and roughly 50 MB
  of Quest VRAM for one hand prop. Cut to 2048 (normal — it carries all the
  moulded relief), 1024 (base colour) and 512 (roughness), for a 2.3 MB file.


What this shell is used for
---------------------------

Unlike the shell it replaced, every control is a separate named mesh
(`Btn_A`, `Btn_B`, `Btn_C_*`, `Btn_L`, `Btn_R`, `Btn_Start`, `Btn_Z`, `DPad`,
`Stick`), so the pad's buttons are ANIMATED by binding those meshes rather than
by authoring primitive stand-ins on top of a static shell.
Scripts/Objects/controllers/n64_controller.gd does the binding;
Tools/input/n64_button_probe.tscn checks it.

The shell also models the expansion bay. Tools/input/n64_pak_probe.tscn measures
that bay out of this file at run time and checks the three paks against it, so
replacing this asset cannot silently leave them hanging in mid-air.
